Heavy Equipment

Excavator / Trackhoe Operator Qualification

OSHA requires employers to instruct each employee in the recognition and avoidance of unsafe conditions and the regulations applicable to their work (29 CFR 1926.21(b)(2)); excavators fall under 1926.602(b), Excavating and other equipment. EM 385-1-1 (2014) 18.A.01 goes further: operators must be trained, qualified, and designated by the employer in writing. We deliver the training, evaluation, and that written qualification record.

  • Documented operator qualification record
  • Swing radius & load capacity charts

  • Hands-on practical evaluation

Powered Industrial Trucks

Powered Industrial Truck Operator Certification (Forklifts & Telehandlers)

OSHA classifies forklifts and telehandlers together as powered industrial trucks — training per 29 CFR 1926.602(c)–(d), which incorporates 1910.178(l). EM 385-1-1 (2014) 18.G.29.b permits only trained, qualified/certified, and designated operators, requires qualification in writing, and requires classroom plus practical training on the same type of truck used on the job. (Section 16.Q adds requirements when PITs hoist rigged loads.)

Aerial Work Platforms

MEWP Boom & Scissor Lift Operator

OSHA regulates these differently: boom lifts are aerial lifts under 29 CFR 1926.453, which permits only authorized persons to operate them (453(b)(2)(ii)); scissor lifts are mobile scaffolds under 1926.451 and 1926.452(w). Training for both falls under 1926.454, delivered by a person qualified in the subject matter. EM 385-1-1 (2014) 22.L.04 requires documented general and equipment-specific familiarization for all aerial work platform operators. Aligned with ANSI A92.

Competent Person

Excavation & Trenching Competent Person

OSHA defines a competent person as one capable of identifying existing and predictable hazards, with authorization to take prompt corrective measures (29 CFR 1926.650(b)), and requires daily excavation inspections (1926.651(k)(1)). EM 385-1-1 (2014) 25.A.02 requires the CP to inspect daily, before each shift, and after every rainstorm — and to demonstrate training, experience, and knowledge of soil analysis, protective systems, and Subpart P (25.A.02.b). This course trains and documents that designation.

  • Competent person designation record
  • Soil classification & testing
  • Sloping, benching, shoring & shielding
  • Daily inspection requirements

Fall Protection

Fall Protection Training — Authorized Person / End User

OSHA requires a training program for each employee who might be exposed to fall hazards (29 CFR 1926.503(a)) and a written certification record with the employee's name, training dates, and trainer's signature (1926.503(b)(1)). EM 385-1-1 (2014) 21.C.05 requires each exposed worker to be trained by a Competent Person before using fall protection equipment. We deliver the training and issue exactly that record.

  • Written certification record per 1926.503(b)(1)
  • ABCDs of fall protection
  • Free-fall distance calculation
  • Harness fitting practice
